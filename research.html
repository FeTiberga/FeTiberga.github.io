Second-Year Paper · 2026

The Anachronistic Testing-Date Bias of Machine Learning Strategies

Federico Tiberga · London Business School

A large literature claims that machine learning methods generate substantial alpha in equity markets. We show that a systematic methodological flaw — using information available only at the testing date to construct training samples — inflates reported performance across the board. We term this the anachronistic testing-date bias. After correcting for it, the out-of-sample edge of most published ML strategies shrinks dramatically, and many reverse sign. Our findings call for stricter backtesting protocols and cast doubt on whether the ML revolution has genuinely reached finance.

Research Interests
Machine Learning in Finance
Evaluating the real predictive power of ML-based asset pricing strategies and the methodological pitfalls that inflate backtested performance.
Empirical Asset Pricing
Cross-sectional return predictability, factor models, and the challenge of distinguishing genuine risk premia from data artifacts.
Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
How firms and investors make choices when outcomes are ambiguous, and how operations research methods can improve those decisions.
Algorithmic Strategy Evaluation
Developing rigorous frameworks for testing quantitative strategies so that in-sample success is not mistaken for out-of-sample alpha.
